## Full (~290 words)

**Memory Palace** — *Organize your mind in the world it already knows.*

Your brain is great at places and terrible at lists. The method of loci — the memory
palace — has exploited that for 2,500 years, which makes it the oldest organizational
technology there is: put vivid imagery at places along a route, then walk the route in
your mind. Memory Palace, an AR trainer for [SPECS](https://www.specs.com/), makes the
palace literal: your real room is the organizing structure, your real walk is the
retrieval, and gems mark each memory.

Speak something worth remembering and a gem drops where you're looking, holding a photo
of the spot, your words, and a spoken recital. One tap conjures it: a mnemonic router
(one LLM call) rewrites your words as bizarre, vivid imagery and generates it as an
animated 3D object (Snap3D) or image (Imagen 3) that cracks out of the gem — "buy milk
for Thursday" became a crowned cow juggling milk bottles on a calendar trampoline. A
violet→teal ribbon threads the loci into an ordered journey.

**Explore** is a read-only walk: distant memories twinkle as anonymous glints and whisper
their contents aloud as you near. **Train** hides everything and quizzes you along the
route — a spatial ping leads you to each locus, you say what lives there, reveal,
self-grade — and the interface deliberately vanishes as mastery grows: words, then object
only, then a blurred photo hint, then bare glow. This organizer's goal is to make itself
unnecessary: the palace ends up in your head. Head-mounted memory palaces measurably beat
desktop recall (Krokos et al., 2018).

Built 100% agentically — Claude Code drove Lens Studio over MCP for design, code, scene
construction, procedural solfeggio sound, self-verification by injected preview gestures,
and even filming the demo footage. Every prompt, dead end, and 2 AM bug hunt:
[PROMPTLOG.md](PROMPTLOG.md).
